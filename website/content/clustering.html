1ml / Clustering

Explore structure

Find groups.
Then question them.

A clean-looking separation is a starting point. Useful clusters need a purpose, sensible features and stability.

Ask what the groups are for

Clustering has no known target label to predict. Start by explaining the intended use: exploring customer behavior, organizing similar items or finding unusual observations. A group should help answer a question rather than merely fill a chart.

Inspect the features first. Missingness, extreme values and large differences in scale can dominate distances. An identifier may produce apparent structure without a meaningful relationship to the task.

A prompt to start with

Use 1ml to investigate customer segments in customers.csv. Clarify how the segments would be used. Inspect missingness, outliers and redundant features, then justify scaling and the distance measure. Compare reasonable clustering methods and check stability under resampling or small perturbations. Explain the groups using measured feature patterns and discuss whether they support the intended use. Produce a concise executed notebook.

Look beyond one internal score

An internal score such as silhouette describes a property of the chosen representation and distance. It does not establish that the groups correspond to real categories or useful customer segments.

  • Compare features and scales against the question being asked.
  • Inspect sensitivity to seeds, samples and reasonable parameter choices.
  • Describe small groups and outliers without assigning unsupported labels.
  • Check whether group descriptions are understandable and useful to the intended audience.

Keep interpretation separate from evidence

Name groups from observed properties, not invented personalities or causal explanations. A two-dimensional projection is useful for exploration, but separation in that plot alone is not validation of the original-space grouping.

Useful output: relevant EDA, a justified representation, a stability check and a plain explanation of what the groups do and do not support.

Use explore, prepare and the clustering reference for a focused investigation.
