Predict a category

Classification starts with the label.

Customer churn, equipment failure or document categories: define the decision before choosing a classifier.

Bring the prediction context

Your agent needs more than a target column. Explain what one row means, when a prediction happens and what action follows it. A feature recorded after a customer leaves may describe churn perfectly while being useless for predicting it.

  • Labels: define the positive class, observation period and ambiguous outcomes.
  • Entities: say whether customers, machines or sites repeat across rows.
  • Timing: distinguish information available at prediction time from information collected later.
  • Error costs: describe what a false positive and a false negative mean.

A prompt to start with

Use 1ml to investigate customer churn in customers.csv. Clarify the target and prediction time, identify repeated customers and outcome-revealing features, and choose a split matching the intended use. Compare a dummy baseline with justified classifiers. Keep preprocessing inside training folds. Report ranking quality separately from threshold-specific precision and recall, and explain the costly errors. Deliver an executed notebook with measured findings and limitations.

Evaluate the decision you will make

Accuracy can hide poor performance on a rare positive class. Precision and recall describe decisions at a threshold; average precision and ROC AUC describe different aspects of ranking. Select metrics for the problem rather than collecting every score available.

If probabilities drive decisions, inspect calibration. If a threshold is tuned, use training or validation data for that choice and freeze it before evaluating the final test. Keep grouped entities together when the claim concerns unseen entities; use a time-aware design when predicting future outcomes.

Useful output: an honest baseline comparison, a justified split, a relevant confusion matrix or precision-recall view, and examples of errors that affect the intended decision.

Try before connecting real data

The synthetic first project provides a small, controlled classification task. The public disaster-tweet notebook shows a text-classification example. Neither establishes performance on your data.

Relevant skills: evaluation, prepare, train and validate.
